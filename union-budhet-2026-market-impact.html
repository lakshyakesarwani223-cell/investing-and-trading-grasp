Union Budget (2026–27) Impact on Share Market and Global Markets

1. Immediate Impact on Indian Equity Markets

The presentation of India’s Union Budget 2026–27 on 1 February 2026 triggered one of the sharpest sell-offs on Dalal Street in years, with key indices falling significantly during special Budget trading sessions.

Budget-Day Market Shock

The BSE Sensex plunged roughly 1,500+ points on Budget day, and the Nifty 50 dipped close to or below significant technical levels, marking one of the worst Budget-day performances in the past six years.

Major wealth erosion in equities was sparked by investor concerns over certain tax and regulatory changes rather than the broader fiscal framework.

Securities Transaction Tax (STT) Hike

The most cited trigger was the sharp increase in STT — especially on futures and options — which raised trading costs for active, short-term, and derivative strategies.

Markets interpreted this as dampening liquidity and profit potential for some trading segments.

Sectoral Swings on Budget Day

  • IT and tech stocks initially showed pockets of resilience.
  • Defence and infrastructure stocks attracted buying interest on expectations of stronger government capex.
  • Retail and consumer stocks remained mixed depending on fiscal reliefs.

Overall, equities reacted negatively in the short run, not due to weak fundamentals, but because of perceived tightening of market dynamics and higher transactional costs.

2. Underlying Budget Signals for Markets

Fiscal Discipline and Growth Orientation

The government targeted a fiscal deficit of approximately 4.3% of GDP and a gradual reduction in the debt-to-GDP ratio, emphasizing macro-stability.

This balance between fiscal discipline and capex expansion is generally viewed positively by medium- to long-term investors.

Capex-Driven Growth Themes

Capital expenditure was boosted to around ₹12.2 lakh crore — the highest in years — focusing on:

  • Transport infrastructure
  • High-speed rail corridors
  • Logistics and freight networks

Such large capex outlays support capital goods, engineering, and infrastructure companies over the medium term.

Manufacturing, Technology & Strategic Sectors

Strategic thrust areas such as semiconductor ecosystems, renewable energy, rare earth minerals, and biopharma initiatives were highlighted.

These sectors could attract long-term domestic and foreign institutional investors seeking structural growth opportunities.

3. Global Market Ripples and International Investor Sentiment

Foreign Portfolio Flows & Risk Appetite

Global investor sentiment was already under pressure due to rising US yields and geopolitical uncertainty.

Higher trading costs and lack of immediate major reforms initially weighed on foreign portfolio inflows, though long-term growth narratives remain intact.

NRI & Offshore Participation

Expanded investment limits for NRIs and simplified compliance for foreign investors could gradually offset some FPI headwinds.

Broader Global Market Environment

Global equities were influenced by monetary policy divergence, geopolitical uncertainty, and commodity price volatility.

Fixed Income & Currency Markets

Credit rating agencies maintained stable growth projections for India, supporting bond markets and long-term currency stability.

4. Long-Term Narrative vs Short-Term Noise

Near-Term Volatility

Equity benchmarks experienced sharp swings, reflecting tactical repositioning and risk-off sentiment.

Medium to Long-Term Positive Signals

  • Strong infrastructure and capex focus
  • Fiscal clarity and defined deficit strategy
  • Sectoral incentives for manufacturing, technology, and exports

These structural elements support a bullish long-term framework for sustained economic growth and equity market recovery once short-term volatility fades.
